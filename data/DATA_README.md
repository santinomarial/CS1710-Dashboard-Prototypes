# Synthetic data dictionary

> **These tables are synthetic.** They were generated by `generate_synthetic.js` (seed 1710) so the prototypes have something to draw. They are not samples, estimates or approximations of real data, and no pattern in them is a finding. Country names are real; the numbers attached to them are not.

## `static/data/flows_synthetic.csv` (1,820 rows, 50,004 synthetic graduates)

One row is a **synthetic headcount** for one combination. Rows are aggregates, never individuals.

| Column | Values |
|---|---|
| `origin` | India, China, South Korea, Nigeria, Brazil, Canada, All other countries |
| `degree_level` | Bachelor's, Master's, Doctorate |
| `field` | 6 broad fields of study |
| `industry` | 7 industries |
| `occupation` | 16 occupations; **each belongs to exactly one industry** |
| `count` | synthetic integer ≥ 1 (combinations that round to 0 are omitted) |

How the counts were made: origin weight × degree weight × a random per-origin field tilt × a **hand-picked field→industry weight** × an occupation "home field" boost × ±35% noise, scaled to 50,000 and rounded. The field→industry weights were chosen only so that selections visibly change the views. Some are 0 (for example Life & Health Sciences → Finance & Insurance), so the "no matching graduates" states can be demonstrated.

## `static/data/salaries_synthetic.csv` (36 rows)

One row is a **synthetic median salary** for one (field, degree level, group).

| Column | Values |
|---|---|
| `field`, `degree_level` | same categories as the flows table |
| `group` | International, Domestic |
| `median_salary` | USD, rounded to $500 |

How the salaries were made: a hand-picked base per field × a degree multiplier (1, 1.18, 1.38) × ±6% noise gives the domestic value. The international value is the domestic value × ±9% noise. **The sign and size of every gap is random.**

## What these tables do NOT support

- The two tables are **independent**. They share category labels (field, degree level) but do not describe the same people, so no salary can be attributed to an origin, industry or occupation.
- Salary medians cannot be combined across degree levels or fields. The dashboards never average them for display; sorting by a mean is only an ordering.
- There is no time dimension and no sample-size or uncertainty information.

## Candidate real sources (to be verified before any swap)

- NSF National Survey of College Graduates: salary by field, degree and citizenship/visa status
- IIE Open Doors and SEVIS by the Numbers: international students by origin and field
- As far as we know, no public source links origin → field → industry for the same individuals, so a real version of the flow diagram would need either a single linked microdata source or a clear statement that its segments come from different tables.
